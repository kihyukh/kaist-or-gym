# Generated coffee practice demonstrations

These fifteen recordings were generated by `kaist_rl_lab.apps.coffee_expert`, not
submitted by students. They are optional practice data for demonstrating behavior
cloning before the class has collected examples. They deliberately use slower
motions and return the pot upright early, leaving a visible gap for reinforcement
learning to improve. They are controlled teaching examples, not a claim about how
future students will behave.

The task still asks for **700 mL**, and the environment still accepts a final fill
within **40 mL** of that target, with the same spill, flow, and tilt conditions.
The generator adds an early-return allowance of **15, 20, 25, 30, or 18 mL** to its
estimate of coffee that will still pour while the pot returns upright. It starts
returning the pot before an accurate expert would. These values change the recorded controls, not
the environment's target, reward, physics, or success test. The resulting examples
finish at **671.95–686.47 mL**, all successful but deliberately imperfect. Their
durations are **33.34–39.78 seconds**, and each spills less than 0.001 mL.

The classroom uses **1.28 m between the arm bases**, at `(-0.64, 0.10)` and
`(0.64, 0.10)` metres. Each base is 6 cm farther outward than in the earlier 1.16 m
layout. Student attempts, generated examples, policy playback, fine-tuning, and
trajectory replay share this spacing. Recordings store the geometry explicitly;
legacy recordings with no geometry field belong to the earlier 1.16 m layout.

Each recording starts with a distinct classroom pose, an empty cup, 1.2 L in the
pot, and both vessels upright. Four coordinates vary independently and uniformly:
the cup moves horizontally within ±9 cm and vertically within ±5 cm; the pot moves
horizontally within ±11 cm and vertically within ±7 cm. The nominal cup and pot
centers are `(-0.28, 0.28)` and `(0.26, 0.62)` metres. The first example starts near
that nominal pose; the other fourteen seeds spread across the four-dimensional
start range. Policy playback and fine-tuning use the nominal pose for consistent
comparisons. Demonstrations and student attempts use the broader distribution.

The generator issues ordinary six-joint angular-velocity commands through
`InteractiveSession.advance` at 32 Hz. An inverse-kinematics controller positions
the vessels, tilts the pot along a constant-spout path, and returns it upright.
It scales approach commands together to keep the vessels upright. All recorded
states and rewards come from environment steps; the generator does not overwrite
the live simulation. The five
`(approach motor limit, pouring rate in radians/second)` pairs repeat in order:
`(0.72, 0.055)`, `(0.68, 0.053)`, `(0.64, 0.057)`, `(0.70, 0.054)`, and
`(0.66, 0.056)`.

compares discounted return, with a discount of 0.99 per simulated second.

Regenerate with `python -m kaist_rl_lab.apps.coffee_expert` from the repository
root using its installed dependencies. The shared classroom spacing, listed
seeds, five speed pairs, and five early-return allowances reproduce these control
sequences. Metadata such as recording timestamps can differ. The web server reads
the packaged recordings without running the expert. Recordings also store their
initial joint angles, so replay does not depend on later start-sampler changes.

The unchanged nearest-neighbor clone learns only the recorded observation/action
pairs; it has no expert fallback. These fifteen recordings contain **17,473
steps**, with **11,206 unique training samples**. The separate whole-trajectory
held-out action MAE is **0.02542**. From the canonical policy pose (seed 7001), the
clone succeeds with **672.054 mL**, less than 0.001 mL spilled, and **36.0625
seconds**. Under the original environment reward, its undiscounted total is
**27.38892** and discounted return is **20.44806**. The separate RL time score used
for fine-tuning is **51.00507**; it uses a different reward function. Its imperfect pouring accuracy is inherited from the practice data.

The same clone was run closed-loop on twenty unseen classroom starts, seeds
**12000–12019**, at 32 Hz, with the same 1.28 m geometry, 700 mL target, and
60-second limit. **20/20** succeeded. Across those runs, final fill ranged from
**671.899 to 686.221 mL**, spill stayed below **0.055 mL**, and duration ranged
from **34.41 to 38.75 seconds**. Discounted returns of the original environment
reward ranged from **19.89630 to 21.03761**. These are new rollouts, not predictions on stored demonstration frames.

The current **10-iteration policy-search** benchmark was run with seeds **2026,
2027, and 2028**, the fixed canonical pose, and the new RL time score. All three
runs reached the 1.4× speed limit, at iterations **10, 10, and 8**, respectively.
The best policy finished in **27.75 seconds**, compared with the clone's
**36.0625 seconds**: **23.05% faster**. Its RL time score increased from **51.00507
to 62.27926** (**22.10%**).

That faster policy poured **666.372 mL**, with less than 0.001 mL spilled. Its target
error was **33.63 mL**, compared with the clone's **27.95 mL**. This is a real tradeoff:
the unchanged task still accepts ±40 mL, and the time-focused objective prefers the
faster successful pour despite its lower accuracy. All **30 current-policy
evaluations succeeded**; **29 of 30 exploratory candidates succeeded**. The failed
candidate remains in the recorded benchmark. Replaying each best policy through
the instructor runtime reproduced its evaluation metrics exactly.

The [reproducible fine-tuning benchmark](../../../benchmarks/coffee_finetuning/README.md)
compares both current methods and the earlier PPO baseline over these same seeds,
including complete candidate/evaluation histories and both reward objectives.

These measurements show improved speed and RL time score from imperfect recorded
behavior. They do not promise a particular gain on every training seed or student
dataset.
Generated examples are only a proxy for a small class's data. Fifteen human
demonstrations may have different coverage, quality, and consistency, and the
held-out check does not establish robustness outside the tested start range.
